Motive Reconstruction Monitoring

A general approach to monitoring decomposed black-box agents, from Leontiev's Activity Theory Nov 2026

The problem

A harmful task is decomposed into subtasks that are individually benign-looking, and executed by black-box agents we cannot introspect. We may only observe, along the way, what flows through the orchestration channel we own — and act there. Goal: reduce the attack success rate during execution, without breaking legitimate work.

Where harm lives: Activity Theory's answer

Leontiev's central claim: an action's goal never contains the activity's motive. An action's sense comes from its place in a tool-mediated, collective activity. A decomposition attack exploits exactly this gap — which Activity Theory described decades before LLM agents existed.

The approach in one line: don't classify traces — reconstruct the activity (how artifacts assemble toward a prohibited object), then intervene on the mediation: sever a declared input, redact a result, stop the activity. The agents stay untouched, by the theory's own logic: the subject is inaccessible, the instruments are not.

What the theory predicts (testable programme)

H1 goal-level monitors fail on twins (verified above) · H2 activity-level monitors succeed (verified above) · H3 the ordering of observation views follows the AT levels · H4 intervening on mediation ≥ intervening on agents — and agents are inaccessible anyway, which the theory explains rather than laments. The training data's role: not what we fine-tune on, but the substrate that makes the motive reconstruction computable — twins give exact utility, chains give exact counterfactuals, certified graphs give exact interdiction.
